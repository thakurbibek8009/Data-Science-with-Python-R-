# Weather Conditions and Bike-Sharing Demand Analysis

## 1. Project Overview

This project investigates the relationship between weather conditions and hourly bike-sharing demand using Python and statistical analysis.

The objective is to determine whether the mean number of hourly bike rentals is lower during less favorable weather conditions than during clear or partly cloudy conditions.

The project follows a reproducible data analysis workflow, from defining the research question and preregistering the analysis plan to running statistical tests, evaluating robustness, and preparing the final research report.

## 2. Research Question

Among the hourly records in the bike-sharing dataset, is the mean number of bike rentals lower during less favorable weather conditions (weather categories 2–4) than during clear or partly cloudy conditions (category 1)?

### Research Hypotheses

- **Null hypothesis (H₀):** The population mean hourly rental count is equal for the two weather groups.
- **Alternative hypothesis (H₁):** The population mean hourly rental count is lower during less favorable weather conditions.

## 3. Dataset

This project uses the hourly Bike Sharing Dataset from the UCI Machine Learning Repository.

**Dataset source:** https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/275/bike+sharing+dataset

The dataset contains hourly bike rental records and information about weather, time, season, and working-day status.

### Weather Groups

| Group | Weather categories | Observations |
|---|---|---:|
| Clear or partly cloudy | 1 | 11,413 |
| Less favorable weather | 2–4 | 5,966 |
| Total | All eligible records | 17,379 |

The target variable is `cnt`, representing the total number of bike rentals per hour.

## 10. Statistical Methods

### Primary Analysis: Welch's t-test

Welch's independent-samples t-test was used to compare mean hourly bike rentals between the two weather groups.

The test is suitable for comparing two group means without assuming equal population variances.

A 95% confidence interval and Cohen's d effect size were also reported. The supplied primary-test output reports a two-sided p-value.

### Robustness Analysis: OLS Regression

An ordinary least squares regression model was fitted to evaluate the relationship between less favorable weather and rental counts while controlling for:

- Hour of the day
- Season
- Working-day status
- Year

HC3 heteroscedasticity-consistent standard errors were used.

The regression provides an adjusted association, not proof of causation. Because the observations are hourly and may be temporally dependent, the HC3 standard errors alone do not address serial correlation.

## 11. Results

### Descriptive Statistics

| Weather group | Sample size | Mean rentals | Standard deviation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Clear or partly cloudy | 11,413 | 204.87 | 189.49 |
| Less favorable weather | 5,966 | 159.99 | 160.74 |

The less favorable weather group had approximately 44.88 fewer rentals per hour on average.

### Primary Test Results

| Statistic | Result |
|---|---:|
| Mean difference (less favorable − clear/partly cloudy) | −44.88 |
| 95% confidence interval | [−50.24, −39.52] |
| Welch's t-statistic | −16.413 |
| Degrees of freedom | 13,935.52 |
| Two-sided p-value | 5.66 × 10⁻⁶⁰ |
| Cohen's d | −0.249 |

The observed mean difference was statistically significant at the 5% level.

### Robustness Results

| Statistic | Result |
|---|---:|
| Adjusted weather coefficient | −37.89 |
| 95% confidence interval | [−41.29, −34.48] |
| Reported p-value | 2.57 × 10⁻¹⁰⁵ |
| Number of observations | 17,379 |
| R-squared | 0.647 |

The adjusted regression also showed a negative association between less favorable weather and hourly rentals.

### Main Finding

Both analyses found lower hourly bike rentals under less favorable weather conditions. The association remained after controlling for the selected time and calendar variables.

However, the study is observational and does not establish that weather alone caused the difference. The regression also showed evidence of residual autocorrelation, which should be considered when interpreting statistical uncertainty.

## 12. Limitations

1. The observational design does not establish causation.
2. Unmeasured factors, such as holidays, events, and local travel patterns, may influence rental demand.
3. Hourly observations may not be independent over time. The reported Durbin–Watson statistic was 0.448, indicating substantial positive residual autocorrelation.
4. Combining weather categories 2–4 may hide differences between individual weather conditions.
5. The findings may not generalize to all cities or bike-sharing systems.

A further sensitivity analysis using a method that explicitly accounts for temporal dependence would strengthen the robustness assessment.

## 14. Conclusion

This project analyzed 17,379 hourly bike-sharing observations to investigate whether rentals were lower during less favorable weather conditions.

The primary analysis estimated 44.88 fewer rentals per hour in the less favorable weather group. The adjusted regression estimated 37.89 fewer rentals per hour after controlling for selected time and calendar variables.

Both analyses support a negative association between less favorable weather and hourly bike rental demand in the analyzed dataset. The conclusions should be interpreted in light of the observational design and potential temporal dependence.
